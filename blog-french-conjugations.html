Irregular Verb Conjugations: Etre, Avoir, Aller Sprints

JD
Jean-Luc Dubois
French Pedagogy Director & Sorbonne Alumnus
April 12, 2026 7 Min Read French Vocabulary

In French, irregular verbs are the undisputed workhorses of daily conversation. Among them, three verbs stand tall as the absolute cornerstones of the language: être (to be), avoir (to have), and aller (to go). Not only are they vital for conveying basic states and locations, but they also serve as auxiliary verbs to build complex tenses like the *Passé Composé* and *Futur Proche*.

Because they are irregular, standard spelling rules do not apply. However, memorizing these conjugation sprints doesn't have to rely on dry, repetitive drills. By applying high-frequency audio melodies and clear sentence structures, you can automate your present-tense controls instantly.

The Conjugation Cheat Sheet

Let's map out the authentic present-tense conjugations for these three crucial verbs side by side to observe their spelling variations:

Pronoun Être (To Be) Avoir (To Have) Aller (To Go)
Je (I) suis ai vais
Tu (You) es as vas
Il / Elle (He/She) est a va
Nous (We) sommes avons allons
Vous (You plural) êtes avez allez
Ils / Elles (They) sont ont vont

"To speak French is to conjugate. Mastering Être, Avoir, and Aller unlocks 70% of the daily conversational grammar needed to reach conversational comfort."

Jean-Luc Dubois, French Pedagogy Director

The Danger Zone: Avoir (ont) vs. Être (sont)

One of the most frequent errors for A1 and A2 French scholars is visual or auditory confusion between ils ont (they have) and ils sont (they are). A simple spelling mistake changes your statement from "they have a dog" to "they are a dog". Pay close attention to the pronunciation liaison!

Correct Pronunciation Liaison
Ils ont un chien. [ils-z-ont]
Translation: They have a dog.

The trailing 's' of 'ils' blends with the vowel of 'ont' to produce a soft 'Z' sound. It clearly signals the verb 'avoir'.

Mispronounced / Incorrect Flow
Ils sont un chien. [ils-s-ont]
Translation: They are a dog.

Pronouncing a sharp 'S' sound links directly to 'sont' (être). Be mindful of your vocal vibration during active conversations!

Fluentra Pro-Tip: The Rhythmic Song Key

Conjugate aloud with a steady beat. Reciting the plural sets: "nous sommes, vous êtes, ils sont" like a rhythmic song hardwires the spelling metrics and silent letters directly into your auditory memory.

Rule of Liaison and Silent Consonants

Remember that in French, trailing consonants are almost always silent unless followed by a vowel. For example, the 's' in tu es, tu as, and tu vas is never pronounced in isolation, keeping your pronunciation smooth and fluid.

Irregular Verbs Key Takeaways

  • Auxiliary foundations: Être and Avoir are the structural pillars for building past-tense phrases.
  • Future Proche via Aller: Combine 'Aller' in the present tense with an infinitive to talk about future actions.
  • Watch Dative/Pronoun liaisons: Soft 'Z' sound means having; sharp 'S' sound means being.
  • Practice audio sprints: Use high-frequency micro-pod recordings weekly to build subconscious auditory memory.
